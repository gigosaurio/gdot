G. prototypes three ways the tank could work · pick by feel

Same keyboard, same creatures, three different games. Each one is small on purpose: play five minutes of each and notice which one you think about when you stop. The question for each: does a press give you something to reason with, the way a Minesweeper number does?

A · Feel

Still water, hidden creatures. An arm feels how many creature keys touch it: one number. Fill every key they do not hold.

Minesweeper on a keyboard, with shapes (the eel is three keys), habitats (jellyfish at the surface) and a starfish that lights its ring. No guessing, ever.

play A

B · Senses

Still water, hidden creatures, but no counting: a crab is a claw, a jellyfish a sting (sharp or faint), the shark a pull along its row.

Each creature is its own little logic puzzle; the keys they share tie them together. More to learn per creature, more to deduce per press.

play B

C · Tides

Nothing hidden: creatures swim fixed rounds and the next five ticks are drawn on every key. Fill the tank in the fewest presses.

The current rules with the simulation taken out of your head. Planning, not deduction. The control to compare A and B against.

play C
